All right... first of all, we have to clear up what a fraction is telling us. And it's simple... they're just division. The fraction $\frac{2}{3}$ is literally just two divided by three. $$\frac{2}{3} = 2 \div 3$$

So, if I get something like $\frac{2}{3}$ for my answer, all I have to do is take my calculator and divide two by three in order to get my final answer. $$\frac{2}{3} = 2 \div 3 = 0.667$$

On another note... what happens when you divide anything by 1? Well, if you're looking closely you might notice that it's the exact same number. $$3 \div 1 = 3$$
So, since fractions are just dividing, we can represent ANYTHING as a fraction if we just put it over one. $$\frac{3}{1} = 3$$ 
In other words, anything over one is just... itself. $$\frac{5}{1} = 5  \space\space \frac{7}{1} = 7$$
And anything is just itself over one. $$7 = \frac{7}{1}$$

Alright, now let's talk a little bit about basic operations with fractions... and luckily, there are only two of them you really need to know for this course.

First, let's learn how to multiply fractions, and we'll pull up an example for this one. Say we have to multiply... $$\frac{4}{5} \times \frac{3}{7}$$
All we really have to do here is multiply right across. What I mean by that is multiply the top numbers together, and then multiply the bottom numbers together. Four times three is twelve, which will be our top number. Five times seven is thirty-five, which will be our bottom number. $$\frac{4}{5} \times \frac{3}{7} = \frac{4 \times 3}{5 \times 7} = \frac{12}{35}$$

So, our final answer is $\frac{12}{35}$. And if you want, you can divide those numbers to get an answer of ~0.344.

Cool thing about this is that you can actually do this with multiple fractions at once. Take a look at this, for example: $$\frac{3}{4} \times \frac{2}{5} \times \frac{7}{6}$$

I can do the same process here of multiplying straight across on the top and the bottom. $3 * 2 * 7 = 42$ and $4 * 5 * 6 = 120$. So, our answer is $\frac{42}{120}$, or 0.35.

Alright, that's enough about multiplying fractions, how about dividing? What's the process there? It's actually pretty simple... take a look at this. $$\frac{3}{5} \div \frac{2}{6}$$For problems like these, all you have to remember is a pretty simple mantra: "keep, change, flip." What it's telling you is to keep the first fraction, change the division sign to a multiplication sign, and flip the second fraction upside down. Like this $$\frac{3}{5} \div \frac{2}{6} \longrightarrow \frac{3}{5} \times \frac{6}{2}$$Once we've changed all the division signs, we can do the same process that we did when we multiply fractions: multiplying straight across! So, our answer is $\frac{18}{10}$, or $1.8$.

You can do this multiple times, too. Let's say we're trying to solve this: $$ \frac{4}{7} \div \frac{8}{3} \div \frac{5}{2}$$
All you have to do is go down the line to get rid of all the division signs by keeping, changing, and flipping. So, I'll start from the left and keep $\frac{4}{7}$, change the division sign, and flip $\frac{8}{3}$. $$ \frac{4}{7} \div \frac{8}{3} \div \frac{5}{2} \longrightarrow  \frac{4}{7} \times \frac{3}{8} \div \frac{5}{2} $$

I'm gonna keep going down the line and change that second division sign, so I'll keep $\frac{3}{8}$, change the division sign, and flip $\frac{5}{2}$. $$\frac{4}{7} \times \frac{3}{8} \div \frac{5}{2} \longrightarrow \frac{4}{7} \times \frac{3}{8} \times \frac{2}{5}$$

Now I can simply multiply straight across! $4 \times 3 \times 2 = 24$, and $7 \times 8 \times 5 = 280$. So, our final answer is $\frac{24}{280}$.

So, now we're able to divide and multiply fractions. Now, let's talk about cross-cancelling, which might just make all your dreams come true, as one bald guy might say. Check this out:$$ \frac{2}{3} \times \frac{5}{4}$$
I could just multiply straight across from here and be done with it, but we can make this problem simpler. 

There are three steps to cross cancelling; the first is to look across the multiplication sign for common factors. So, I might compare the $3$ on the bottom of the first fraction and the five on top of the second fraction. Three and five have no common factors, so I'll ignore those.

But take a look at the two and the four. They have a common factor: it's two! So, I'm gonna divide both of these numbers by this common factor, which is two. $$ \frac{\cancel2}{3} \times \frac{5}{\cancel4} = \frac{1}{3} \times \frac{5}{2}$$

Now the problem is a whole lot simpler, and I can multiply straight across to get $\frac{5}{6}$.

Another small thing to know is simplifying fractions. Let's say I had that previous problem and I just multiplied straight across from the get-go. $$ \frac{2}{3} \times \frac{5}{4} = \frac{10}{12}$$

Something I can do here is make this fraction simpler. In order to do this, all I have to do is find a common factor between the top and bottom number. You can do this by listing the factors of both numbers out: $$ 10 \rightarrow 1, \textbf{2}, 5,10$$$$\\12 \rightarrow 1, \textbf{2}, 3, 4, 6, 12$$
From this, I see that two is a common factor of both, so I'm gonna divide the top and the bottom numbers by two: $$ \frac{10 \div 2}{12 \div 2} = \frac{5}{6} $$
That's how you simplify a fraction, not too hard.
